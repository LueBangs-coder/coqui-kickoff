# Learning design

Coquí Kickoff is a first-week Puerto Rican Spanish practice app for beginners aged 13 or older. Its target is a small set of useful interactions: greet someone, ask for help, introduce family, request food, follow directions, and talk about a football game. Completing a week is practice evidence, not a fluency claim or a formal proficiency assessment.

## A manageable week

Start with about 8–12 minutes of language practice per day, adjusting to the learner's attention and confidence. That duration is a practical starting point, not a research-derived optimum. A suggested routine is to review due phrases, work through one six-phrase lesson, and try a short real conversation with a family member, even if it is just a greeting and a question. Football is freely playable at any time, independently of that routine.

| Day | Theme | Can-do goal | Family follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hola, rookie | Greet someone, introduce yourself, ask for slower speech. | Exchange names and ask how each person is doing. |
| 2 | Game time | Invite someone to play and encourage a teammate. | Take turns using `Es tu turno` during a game. |
| 3 | My people | Introduce family and discuss Puerto Rican identity. | Describe a family photo; change example sentences to fit your family. |
| 4 | Food break | Ask for food and water and recognize familiar Puerto Rican vocabulary. | Ask for a snack or talk about what you like. |
| 5 | Around town | Ask where a place is and follow simple directions. | Give directions to an imaginary park. |
| 6 | Make a play | Understand short commands and encourage another try. | Call a few simple movements during a safe practice drill. |
| 7 | Game day | Talk about the game and reuse the week's language. | Hold a short game-day conversation without reading the cards. |

The seven lessons contain 42 phrases. Each has six phrase-learning cards followed by six Spanish-to-English questions and six English-to-Spanish responses: 84 core answer prompts across the starter week. Each phrase also has context, normal/slow audio, a stress guide, and optional speaking/recording. Missed prompts recur, and spaced reviews are repeatable; there is no second-week or intermediate curriculum yet. All lessons remain available; the learner can repeat one or move at a different pace. The game is an original arcade running challenge, available immediately on mobile or desktop with no lesson prerequisites or play limits. All 30 active 1997 teams are selectable, including the Giants, with a different opponent chosen by the learner. It supplies an enjoyable reason to return, but does not replace speaking with people.

## What the app practices

1. **Meaning before recall.** Each phrase starts with Spanish, an English meaning, a real situation, a syllable/stress guide, and optional speech playback. English support makes the task comprehensible for a beginner.
2. **Both directions.** Spanish-to-English choices check recognition; English-to-Spanish typed responses practice producing the target phrase. These are different skills and both are included.
3. **Feedback and retry.** The correct form is shown after a miss, and missed prompts return. A mistake does not remove earned XP or end a session. Multiple attempts are practice, not cheating.
4. **Optional speaking.** Say the phrase aloud, listen again, and optionally record a short attempt for self-comparison. The app does not assign an automated pronunciation score.
5. **Spaced review.** Correct retrieval on or after the due date moves a phrase to a review after 1, 3, then 7 days. Further successful spaced reviews stay at 7 days. Early repeats do not skip those intervals. A miss resets the phrase to today; correcting it schedules tomorrow. Missed days remain overdue rather than being discarded.

The 1/3/7-day schedule is a simple product choice informed by spacing research, not a validated optimal schedule for every learner. Practice dates use the device's local date; review intervals use calendar-day arithmetic to avoid daylight-saving errors. The app does not punish a missed day.

Correct answers earn 10 XP. A completed lesson or due-review session earns 30 XP, including intentional replays. Lesson completion is recorded once; the session controller must award the session XP once, even if the finish button is pressed repeatedly. XP is encouragement rather than a measure of Spanish proficiency and never restricts football access.

## Research behind these choices

Sources were consulted on September 21, 2026. These sources support the general learning principles. They do not establish that this particular app, football game, daily duration, or fixed review schedule produces a measured outcome.

| Principle | Source and evidence | Application here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieve and revisit | The U.S. Institute of Education Sciences practice guide rates repeated retrieval of key content as strongly supported and distributing study over time as moderately supported. [IES: Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) | Recall in both directions, revisit missed answers, and schedule later reviews. |
| Make input understandable | ACTFL recommends meaningful target-language use supported by context, visuals, and checks for understanding. [ACTFL: Facilitate Target Language Use](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/guiding-principles-for-language-learning/facilitate-target-language-use) | Short situational phrases, plain English meanings, optional audio, and small comprehension tasks. |
| Give useful feedback | ACTFL emphasizes feedback tied to the learner's goal, delivered clearly without excessive correction. [ACTFL: Provide Effective Feedback](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/guiding-principles-for-language-learning/provide-effective-feedback) | Show the target phrase, allow another try, and avoid punitive failure screens. |
| Start with useful interactions | Instituto Cervantes describes beginner interaction goals including greetings, introductions, and simple exchanges about familiar topics. [Cervantes: A1–A2 objectives](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/plan_curricular/niveles/01_objetivos_relacion_a1-a2.htm) | Family, food, directions, and personal interests alongside football vocabulary. These lessons do not constitute the full A1 syllabus. |
| Teach sound and stress explicitly | The Cervantes beginner pronunciation inventory includes vowels, syllables, stress, and variation. [Cervantes: A1–A2 pronunciation inventory](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/plan_curricular/niveles/03_pronunciacion_inventario_a1-a2.htm) | Spanish syllable guides and replayable audio; no fabricated accent or speech-accuracy score. |

The app's implementation of these principles is a design judgment, not an endorsement by these organizations. Recognition questions and typed recall are useful practice but cannot establish listening or conversational proficiency by themselves. Short conversations and feedback from Puerto Rican family members are an intentional part of the recommended routine.

## Puerto Rican Spanish and cultural accuracy

Puerto Rican Spanish is a valid variety of Spanish. Regional vocabulary is introduced with a situation and a widely understood equivalent, so a learner can connect family language with language heard elsewhere. Other varieties are not marked as inferior.

- **China** means an orange in the lesson's Puerto Rican food context; `naranja` is also accepted. ASALE explicitly records this fruit meaning for Puerto Rico and the Dominican Republic. [ASALE: china](https://www.asale.org/damer/china)
- **Habichuelas** means beans. The Puerto Rican and Dominican use is recorded by ASALE; the app also accepts `frijoles`. [ASALE: habichuela](https://www.asale.org/damer/habichuela)
- **Guagua** means bus in the bus-stop phrase. In Puerto Rico it can also refer to a larger passenger vehicle, so the app does not teach a single context-free meaning. [ASALE: guagua](https://www.asale.org/damer/guagua)
- **Boricua** is a Puerto Rican identity word and can be used for a person of any gender. [RAE/ASALE: boricua](https://dle.rae.es/boricua)

Gender alternatives such as `Estoy listo` / `Estoy lista` and `Ganamos juntos` / `Ganamos juntas` are included where appropriate. Family examples are sample dialogue, not claims about the learner's relatives. The Puerto Rican flag and coquí imagery celebrate Puerto Rican heritage; the mascot is an original illustration. Puerto Rico's official tourism organization describes the coquí and its distinctive call, while the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service identifies coquíes as part of Puerto Rican natural and cultural heritage. [Discover Puerto Rico: coquí](https://www.discoverpuertorico.com/es/coqui), [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service: coquí guajón](https://www.fws.gov/species/rock-frog-eleutherodactylus-cooki)

## Pronunciation and voice limits

The written pronunciation line keeps Spanish spelling, separates syllables, and uses capitals to highlight word stress. It is a reading aid, not IPA, an English phonetic spelling, or a substitute for listening. Notes introduce silent h, the s sound of z/c before e or i, and a y-like ll pronunciation. Beginners are encouraged to speak clearly rather than imitate a caricature of regional features.

All 42 lesson phrases plus three helpers have bundled MP3 playback using Microsoft's `es-PR-VictorNeural` synthetic Puerto Rican Spanish voice. Clips work without installed system voices and are cached for offline practice. Normal and slower playback preserve pitch. For future text that lacks a bundled recording, the app can use a clearly labelled Spanish browser/OS voice, preferring `es-PR`. A language tag is the provider's label, not evidence that a human Puerto Rican speaker reviewed the recording. A family member is the best available partner for natural local phrasing and pronunciation feedback. [Audio provenance and reproducible generation](AUDIO.md) document the source and limits.

Microphone practice is optional. Recordings stay in memory and are not uploaded by the app. Progress is stored in the current browser; there is no account, cross-device synchronization, advertising, or analytics. Browser/OS speech services may have their own implementation and privacy behavior. The operating guide in [README.md](../README.md) describes the available controls; release and device-validation evidence belongs in [WORKLOG.md](../WORKLOG.md).
